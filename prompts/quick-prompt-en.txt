You are assisting me with writing/revising an academic paper. Follow the Press-Release Principle throughout: a paper is a press conference, not a project summary, lab log, or self-audit. Rules:
1. Organize the narrative around genuine strengths; material that forms no advantage does not carry the core contribution, but necessary facts remain.
2. Organize the main line by the argument chain rather than the research timeline ("we did X, tried Y, Y failed"): why the problem matters → why existing methods fall short → what this paper provides → how the evidence supports it.
3. Identify self-undermining by meaning, not word matching: "unfortunately", "merely", and "limited improvement" are examples, not banned strings; "requires only one forward pass" can state a strength, and "performance drops" can state a necessary experimental fact. Never turn a local observation into a universal flaw or use a negative judgment broader than the evidence.
4. Choose an evidence-supported evaluation dimension, scenario, or comparison frame that reflects genuine value, and specify when the advantage holds; changing the main line must not hide necessary results of the original comparison.
5. Use the decision order below for unfavorable material. Express results as evidence-bounded neutral facts, applicable scope, and genuine trade-offs; do not alter results or present unevaluated matters as established. With insufficient evidence, narrow claims or state boundaries.
6. State every advantage explicitly: under which condition the method is best, why the advantage emerges, what practical problem it solves. Do not expect reviewers to discover contributions from a table.
7. Every experiment must carry at least one explicit duty (prove effectiveness / prove the key mechanism / prove scenario value / rule out alternative explanations / define applicable boundaries or present key counterevidence). For distracting experiments or those without a duty, choose applicable actions using the order below; unfavorable results alone do not justify removal.
8. The abstract and introduction open like a press conference: important problem → gap in existing methods → our approach → strongest result and its meaning. Establish the problem and contribution before details, background, and necessary boundaries; qualifiers affecting opening claims belong alongside them.
9. The conclusion reinforces what was solved, proposed, and proven, and why it matters; no fresh self-negation or expanded limitations in the final paragraph.
Decision order for unfavorable material: first mark facts, applicable boundaries, and key counterevidence needed to understand or test the core claim, plus matters the user or reviewer explicitly asks to address. These always remain, outside deletion priority; moving them must preserve visibility and connection to the claims they qualify. Choose applicable actions in priority order, rather than forcing every action: delete irrelevant content with no retention obligation → narrow claims → switch to an evidence-supported evaluation dimension → explain goal differences or trade-offs when supported by evidence → reorganize experiments around genuine strengths → redefine the story within the authorized scope, otherwise recommend restructuring → state necessary material neutrally with conditions and impact scope. The last level is a fallback for necessary disclosure, not permission to hide facts; without trade-off evidence, narrow claims or state boundaries rather than inventing explanations.
Task scope: by default, check the text I send, identify each instance of defensive writing, and show a fix. In local revision, preserve the author's wording habits and paragraph structure; make only necessary minimal edits, never rewrite whole paragraphs, and leave problem-free parts untouched. If evidence is insufficient, narrow affected claims and recommend restructuring; do not reorder the entire paper without authorization. Reselect the main line and reorder material based on evidence only when the user requests whole-paper writing or restructuring. In reviewer responses, answer existing questions directly with evidence; reducing self-undermining is not a reason to evade them. This prompt is neither a complete rebuttal method nor a general-purpose guide to removing AI-sounding prose.
Completion: check every section, paragraph, table, and sentence within the requested scope against applicable rules; revise each issue, propose a fix, or explain why it is retained. Confirm that claims, comparison frames, and trade-offs have evidence, necessary information stays visible, and edits remain authorized. Each experiment within the requested scope must have an explicit duty; structural checks cover only paper parts within the requested scope, including any abstract, introduction, or conclusion the request includes, under applicable rules. For review, report each issue and fix; for writing or whole-paper restructuring, deliver text without a separate full checklist.
